# FR–FOURCE–FORMAL Sweep Ledger

## 001 — The Definition Problem

Result: generic relational persistence is not novel. Structural stability, persistent topology, computational mechanics, organizational invariance, structural realism, and closely related contemporary persistence frameworks occupy major portions of the space.

Surviving target: representation-robust organizational invariance under constrained transformation.

## 002 — The Redundancy Gauntlet

Result: structural stability, persistent homology, computational mechanics, viability theory, organizational invariance, and structural realism explain major subproblems. A single universal persistence scalar is not justified.

Surviving target: an identity architecture based on equivalence relations, minimal sufficient representations, lineage, and constraint-bounded transformation.

## 003 — The Identity Kernel

Result: type identity and token continuity must be separated. Instantaneous structural similarity is insufficient because perfect copies can be equivalent without being the same continuing token. Identity may be path-dependent.

Working kernel: organizational criterion + admissible transformations + causal/historical lineage.

## 004 — The Reduction Attempt

Result: causal states + bisimulation + viability + lineage reconstruct most operational cases previously attributed to Fource. No new physical primitive is required.

Surviving problem: relevance selection and system-boundary selection.

## 005 — The Emergence Test

Result: Markov blankets, causal emergence, information bottlenecks, renormalization/coarse-graining, and organizational closure can privilege boundaries or scales in important domains, but no single universal boundary-selection rule has been established for arbitrary systems.

Working hypothesis: systemhood may be supported by convergence among multiple independent criteria rather than one unique scale or scalar score.

## 006 — The Convergence Criterion

Result: formulate a testable Convergent Systemhood Hypothesis rather than an ontological claim.

CSH-001: dynamically organized systems should exhibit greater cross-method convergence on candidate organizational partitions than matched null systems; convergence should track organization more strongly than constituent identity.

Required controls:

- matched null systems
- nested organization
- constituent replacement
- relational rewiring
- single-metric decoys
- coupling-strength confound
- label permutation invariance
- observation degradation
- counterfactual interventions where possible
- assumption/provenance dependency graph
