# Convergent Systemhood: Operational Tests for Non-Arbitrary Organizational Persistence Under Transformation

## Abstract

To be written after executable benchmark results exist.

## 1. Problem

Many physical and dynamical systems retain identifiable organization despite changes in constituent realization. This paper does not posit a new physical force or substance. It asks whether organizational levels can be operationally distinguished from arbitrary partitions by convergence among methodologically nonredundant identification procedures.

## 3. Definitions

### Candidate system partition

A partition or coarse-graining of microscopic degrees of freedom proposed as an organizational level.

### Systemhood profile

A multidimensional profile assembled from independently motivated criteria such as statistical boundary strength, predictive sufficiency/compression, causal informativeness, viability, perturbational robustness, and lineage stability where applicable.

### Convergent systemhood

A candidate organizational partition is supported when multiple sufficiently nonredundant identification methods recover it or place it near the top of their rankings more strongly and robustly than expected under matched null models.

### Organizational horizon

The perturbation region over which an organizational partition remains independently recoverable under preregistered criteria. Recoverability must not be conflated with ontic persistence.

## 4. Hypothesis

CSH-001: Dynamically organized systems will produce significantly greater agreement among methodologically nonredundant boundary-, prediction-, causality-, viability-, and robustness-based identification methods than matched null systems, and this convergence will preferentially track organizational architecture rather than microscopic constituent identity.

## 5. Preregistered Predictions

P1. Structured systems show greater cross-method convergence than null systems.

P2. Implanted organizational boundaries are recovered above chance.

P3. Nested systems produce multiple stable nondominated partitions rather than a single mandatory optimum.

P4. Constituent replacement preserving relational architecture disrupts convergence less than comparably sized relational rewiring.

P5. Increasing perturbation eventually produces a reproducible collapse or reorganization of recovery for the original partition.

P6. Decoy partitions optimized for a single criterion do not attract broad convergence.

## 8. Failure Criteria

CSH-001 is weakened or rejected if, under preregistered analysis:

- structured systems do not show convergence above matched nulls;
- recovered partitions do not track implanted organization above chance;
- apparent convergence is explained by a single shared confound such as coupling strength;
- results are unstable under reasonable estimators, relabeling, or resampling;
- constituent-preserving organizational perturbations and organization-destroying perturbations cannot be distinguished;
- the full procedure reduces without loss to one established method and adds no explanatory, classificatory, or predictive value.

No post-hoc appeal to a deeper hidden coherence may rescue a failed preregistered prediction.
