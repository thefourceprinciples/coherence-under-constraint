## Coherence Under Constraint: Foundational Charter and Research Constitution

**Document status:** Draft for ratification  
**Proposed release:** v0.2.0-draft.1  
**Date:** 2026-07-17  
**Canonical Garden identity:** G01-T003  
**Legacy identity:** FR-003  
**Return phrase:** *Return to coherence under constraint.*

## Charter declaration

Coherence Under Constraint, abbreviated **CUC**, is a framework for studying when an organized system preserves, loses, recovers, or transforms its organization while operating under limitations, perturbations, boundaries, flows, and internal tensions.

Its canonical root invariant is:

> **A structure persists when its organization survives the constraints acting upon it.**

The earlier formulation remains historically valid as the seed from which the present framework grew:

> **Structure persists when coherence survives constraint.**

The revised invariant is preferred because it does not define persistence by coherence alone. It allows coherence to be measured as one contributor to persistence while organization, viability, boundary integrity, throughput, and disturbance remain independently observable.

CUC is not declared here to be a universal physical law. It is a formal research framework, a model-building discipline, and a family of testable hypotheses. Its claims acquire authority only through explicit definitions, valid derivations, reproducible computation, controlled observation, and successful prediction.

This Charter establishes:

1. the conceptual root that must be preserved;
2. the ontology and notation of the framework;
3. the separation between local coherence generation and global persistence;
4. the formal roles of Fource, Darkness, return, boundary, and throughput;
5. the difference between definitions, hypotheses, reduced models, metaphors, and applications;
6. the standards by which claims may be tested, promoted, revised, quarantined, or retired;
7. the research program required to advance CUC from a coherent framework into an empirically disciplined theory.

# Part I — Purpose, scope, and authority

## 1. Purpose

CUC exists to answer a family of related questions:

- What exactly persists when a system changes?
- Which relationships constitute the organization of the system?
- Which constraints are merely restrictive, and which enable stable organization?
- How is local coordination generated?
- When does local coordination produce global coherence?
- How do throughput, noise, dissipation, boundary quality, and interface mismatch affect survival?
- What distinguishes resilient coherence from brittle synchrony, forced alignment, stagnation, or lock-in?
- When a system is displaced, can it return without merely repeating its microscopic state?
- Which observations would show that a CUC model is wrong?

The framework is intended to be portable across domains, but portability must be earned through explicit translation. A biological, computational, social, cognitive, engineering, or cosmological application is not accepted merely because the language of coherence seems evocative. Each application must identify its system, state, organization, constraints, observables, perturbations, time horizon, failure conditions, and falsifiers.

## 2. Scope

CUC addresses systems that possess, at minimum:

- distinguishable states or trajectories;
- a boundary or operational scope;
- an identifiable organization or relational pattern;
- constraints on admissible states or transitions;
- a time horizon over which persistence can be assessed;
- observable success and failure conditions.

CUC may be instantiated as:

- a mathematical model;
- a dynamical simulation;
- an experimental protocol;
- a diagnostic framework;
- an engineering design method;
- a comparative explanatory model.

CUC does not, by itself, establish:

- that coherence is a fundamental force of nature;
- that all persistent structures are phase oscillators;
- that coherence is morally good;
- that persistence proves truth, intelligence, agency, or consciousness;
- that metaphorical similarity demonstrates causal equivalence;
- that one scalar metric captures all relevant organization;
- that a simulation validates a real-world domain;
- that speculative applications are established merely because they can be expressed in CUC vocabulary.

## 3. Canonical authority

Every CUC statement must carry one of the following statuses.

| Status | Meaning | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| **Root invariant** | The organizing commitment of the framework | Preserved unless the framework itself is renamed or replaced |
| **Definition** | A stipulated meaning needed for formal work | Canonical within its declared model version |
| **Axiom or modeling commitment** | A condition adopted for a class of CUC models | Must be explicit and may be revised |
| **Derived result** | A statement following from declared definitions and assumptions | Valid only within those assumptions |
| **Hypothesis** | A testable expectation not yet established | Requires a falsifier and evidence status |
| **Reduced model** | A simplified predictive or explanatory approximation | Must state its regime and limitations |
| **Empirical finding** | A result supported by specified data or experiment | Must identify the protocol and uncertainty |
| **Application mapping** | A translation of CUC into a domain | Does not validate the trunk by itself |
| **Metaphor or mnemonic** | A conceptual aid | Cannot bear evidentiary weight |
| **Speculative branch** | A proposal lacking adequate formal or empirical support | Must remain labeled |
| **Quarantined claim** | A claim withheld from promotion because prerequisites are missing | Cannot be cited as established CUC theory |
| **Deprecated claim** | A statement retained for history but no longer recommended | Must name its replacement |
| **Falsified claim** | A sufficiently precise claim contradicted within its stated regime | Must not be silently reformulated after failure |

No application, simulation, whitepaper, registry entry, or software implementation may silently promote its own status.

## 4. Version authority

This document is a proposed reformalization. Until ratified, it does not erase the earlier Garden record. Ratification should create a new semantic version while preserving provenance.

The recommended version structure is:

- **Patch change:** clarification, typographic repair, citation repair, or implementation correction that does not alter a definition.
- **Minor change:** new compatible metric, derived model, experiment, or application protocol.
- **Major change:** alteration of the root invariant, foundational ontology, or meaning of a canonical primitive.

The stable Garden identifier for CUC remains **G01-T003**. **FR-003** remains a legacy alias. Any proposed GFR migration should be treated as registry infrastructure until it is consistently reflected across the Almanac, repositories, manuscripts, and application records.

# Part II — The conceptual root

## 5. The seed and its maturation

The earliest CUC seed was expressed as a compact intuition:

```latex
\[
S = C \times M
\tag{HIST-1}
\]
```

Here, structure was imagined as an interaction between coherence and constraint. This was generative but under-specified. The symbols did not identify a system, a time horizon, an organization, a measurement procedure, or a failure condition.

A later mnemonic added throughput:

```latex
\[
S = C \times M \times J
\tag{HIST-2}
\]
```

This captured an important insight: many structures persist only because energy, matter, information, attention, or resources continue to pass through them. Yet the expression still implied that more throughput is always better, did not distinguish beneficial from destructive constraints, and did not ensure that the resulting quantity was measurable or dimensionally coherent.

The present Charter preserves the intuition while replacing the mnemonic with an explicit hierarchy:

1. **Organization** identifies what must be preserved.
2. **Constraint** defines admissible states and transitions.
3. **Coherence** measures selected relations within or across the system.
4. **Fource** models a possible local generator of coherence.
5. **Throughput adequacy** measures whether flow lies in a viable operating region.
6. **Darkness** aggregates modeled sources of degradation or obstruction.
7. **Persistence** measures survival of organization over a specified horizon.
8. **Return** measures recovery after displacement.

## 6. The central distinction

The essential reformalization is this:

> **Coherence is not persistence. Coherence is an observable or family of observables that may contribute to persistence.**

A system may be highly coherent and still fail. Examples include brittle synchronization, pathological lock-in, common-mode collapse, externally forced alignment, and coherent behavior directed toward destructive ends.

A system may also preserve organization without high global synchrony. Modular systems, asynchronous protocols, ecologies, markets, neural systems, and fault-tolerant networks may depend on local coordination, controlled heterogeneity, phase offsets, or role differentiation.

CUC therefore distinguishes:

- microscopic state from macroscopic organization;
- organization from viability;
- local coherence from global coherence;
- coherence generation from coherence persistence;
- persistence from return;
- descriptive coherence from ethical value.

# Part III — Formal domain and primitives

## 7. Model declaration

A CUC model must declare a model tuple containing at least the following objects:

```latex
\[
\mathfrak{C}
=
\left(
\mathcal{S},
\mathcal{E},
\mathcal{X},
\mathcal{B},
\mathcal{O},
\mathcal{M},
\mathcal{J},
\mathcal{P},
T,
\Pi,
\mathcal{Q}
\right)
\tag{CUC-1}
\]
```

where:

| Object | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `\mathcal{S}` | the system under study |
| `\mathcal{E}` | the relevant environment |
| `\mathcal{X}` | the state space |
| `\mathcal{B}` | the boundary or inclusion rule |
| `\mathcal{O}` | the organization map |
| `\mathcal{M}` | the constraint set or constraint operator |
| `\mathcal{J}` | the throughput variables and accounting rules |
| `\mathcal{P}` | the perturbation family |
| `T` | the observation horizon |
| `\Pi` | the distribution or protocol governing initial conditions and perturbations |
| `\mathcal{Q}` | the observables, estimators, and quality criteria |

A model that omits one of these objects must explain why it is unnecessary or unobservable in that domain.

## 8. System and environment

A **system** is the selected collection of components, relations, and processes whose organization is being evaluated. The **environment** contains relevant influences not included as system components.

The distinction is operational rather than metaphysically absolute. A valid CUC analysis must state why a boundary is drawn where it is and how sensitive the conclusions are to moving that boundary.

Let the system state be:

```latex
\[
x(t) \in \mathcal{X}
\tag{CUC-2}
\]
```

and the relevant environmental state be:

```latex
\[
e(t) \in \mathcal{E}
\tag{CUC-3}
\]
```

The joint dynamics may be written abstractly as:

```latex
\[
\dot{x}(t)
=
f\!\left(
x(t),
e(t),
u(t),
\mathcal{M}(t),
\xi(t)
\right)
\tag{CUC-4}
\]
```

where `u(t)` denotes modeled inputs or controls and `\xi(t)` denotes unresolved disturbance or stochastic forcing.

## 9. Boundary

A **boundary** determines what is included in the system and regulates exchange with the environment. Boundaries may be spatial, logical, energetic, informational, organizational, legal, semantic, or operational.

A boundary is not assumed to be impermeable. In many persistent systems, boundary selectivity matters more than closure.

The boundary should be represented by an inclusion rule, exchange operator, or interface map. A generic exchange relation is:

```latex
\[
\dot{q}(t)
=
J_{\mathrm{in}}(t)
-
J_{\mathrm{out}}(t)
+
G(t)
-
L(t)
\tag{CUC-5}
\]
```

where `q(t)` is a conserved or tracked quantity, `G(t)` is internal generation, and `L(t)` is loss or consumption.

Boundary quality must be evaluated through leakage, selectivity, latency, impedance, error, and failure modes appropriate to the domain.

## 10. Organization

**Organization** is the set of relations, invariants, roles, functions, or macroscopic patterns whose preservation defines continued identity for the purpose of the study.

An organization map projects a microscopic state into an organizational description:

```latex
\[
O(t)
=
\mathcal{O}\!\left(x(t)\right)
\tag{CUC-6}
\]
```

Persistence normally does not require exact microscopic recurrence. Instead, the model defines an equivalence class around a reference organization:

```latex
\[
[O_0]_{\varepsilon_O}
=
\left\{
O:
d_O(O,O_0)
\leq
\varepsilon_O
\right\}
\tag{CUC-7}
\]
```

The metric `d_O` and tolerance `\varepsilon_O` must be declared before outcomes are inspected whenever feasible. Choosing them after observing failure can make persistence unfalsifiable.

An organization map may be:

- structural, such as graph topology or modular arrangement;
- functional, such as input-output competence;
- dynamical, such as an attractor family or return map;
- informational, such as preserved coding relations;
- causal, such as a stable intervention-response pattern;
- composite, combining several of these.

## 11. Viability

The **viability region** contains states compatible with continued operation under declared requirements:

```latex
\[
\mathcal{V}(t)
\subseteq
\mathcal{X}
\tag{CUC-8}
\]
```

Organization and viability are distinct. A state can remain viable while its organization changes, and an organization can remain recognizable while approaching a viability boundary.

The instantaneous viability indicator is:

```latex
\[
I_V(t)
=
\mathbf{1}
\left\{
x(t)\in\mathcal{V}(t)
\right\}
\tag{CUC-9}
\]
```

## 12. Constraint

A **constraint** restricts or shapes the admissible states, transitions, exchanges, or transformations of a system. Constraints may prevent motion, but they may also create the channels through which organized motion becomes possible.

For a state `x` at time `t`, let the admissible transition set be:

```latex
\[
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{M}}(x,t)
=
\left\{
v:
g_k(x,v,t)\leq 0,
\quad
h_\ell(x,v,t)=0
\right\}
\tag{CUC-10}
\]
```

Constraints should be classified along several axes:

| Axis | Categories |
|---|---|
| Origin | endogenous, exogenous, jointly produced |
| Form | hard, soft, probabilistic |
| Time | static, dynamic, adaptive, intermittent |
| Function | enabling, restrictive, protective, routing, stabilizing, destabilizing |
| Location | component, interface, boundary, environment |
| Resource | energetic, material, informational, temporal, spatial, computational |
| Topology | connectivity, modularity, hierarchy, geometry |
| Governance | rule, protocol, norm, permission, prohibition |

Constraint magnitude alone is not a sufficient predictor of persistence. The framework therefore uses **constraint fitness**, not raw constraint strength, when describing how a constraint architecture supports an organization.

Let `\mathcal{G}_O(x,t)` denote transitions that preserve or restore the declared organization. A local constraint-fitness measure may be defined as:

```latex
\[
\chi_{\mathcal{M}}(x,t)
=
\frac{
\mu\!\left(
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{M}}(x,t)
\cap
\mathcal{G}_O(x,t)
\right)
}{
\mu\!\left(
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{M}}(x,t)
\right)
}
\tag{CUC-11}
\]
```

when the measure `\mu` is defined and the denominator is nonzero. This quantity lies between zero and one, but it is model-relative and may be difficult to estimate. Alternative estimators are permitted if they preserve the distinction between constraint intensity and constraint fitness.

## 13. Throughput

**Throughput** is the rate at which a relevant quantity passes through, is transformed by, or is made available to the system. Depending on the domain it may represent energy, matter, information, work, capital, attention, nutrients, messages, or another explicitly named quantity.

Raw throughput is written `J(t)`. CUC does not assume that persistence increases monotonically with `J(t)`. Starvation and overload can both destroy organization. The preferred variable is therefore a normalized **throughput adequacy function**:

```latex
\[
\psi_J(t)
=
\psi\!\left(
J(t);
J_{\min}(t),
J_{\star}(t),
J_{\max}(t)
\right)
\in [0,1]
\tag{CUC-12}
\]
```

A convenient but non-canonical example is a Gaussian operating-window model:

```latex
\[
\psi_J(t)
=
\exp\!\left[
-\frac{
\left(J(t)-J_{\star}(t)\right)^2
}{
2s_J^2
}
\right]
\tag{CUC-13}
\]
```

Other domains may require asymmetric, thresholded, saturating, hysteretic, or multivariate adequacy functions.

## 14. Dissipation and loss

Dissipation is not automatically harmful. Many systems require dissipation to remove heat, settle transients, suppress runaway modes, or export entropy. CUC therefore distinguishes **necessary dissipation** from **excess or misdirected loss**.

Let `\lambda_{\mathrm{bad}}(t)` represent the normalized portion of dissipation or loss that degrades the declared organization. It must not be substituted for total physical dissipation without justification.

## 14.1 Entropy, disorder, and coherence

CUC does not identify thermodynamic entropy with incoherence or Darkness. Entropy has precise domain-dependent definitions. Phase disorder, uncertainty, missing information, organizational degradation, and thermodynamic entropy may correlate in a particular model, but they are not interchangeable by default.

Any entropy claim must specify:

- the state ensemble or probability distribution;
- the coarse-graining;
- whether entropy is thermodynamic, Shannon, von Neumann, algorithmic, or another defined quantity;
- whether the system is open or closed;
- the boundary fluxes;
- the units and reference measure;
- the derivation linking entropy to the selected coherence or persistence observable.

The engineering aim of a coherence-preserving boundary is not literal zero entropy. It is to control relevant entropy production, export, accumulation, or drift while maintaining organization and viability.

## 15. Perturbation

A **perturbation** is a declared displacement, disturbance, intervention, or change in conditions used to test survival or return.

The perturbation family is:

```latex
\[
p
\in
\mathcal{P}
\tag{CUC-14}
\]
```

and the experimental or modeled perturbation protocol is governed by `\Pi`. A persistence claim without a perturbation regime is incomplete because nearly any structure can be described as persistent under sufficiently gentle conditions.

Perturbations should be characterized by:

- amplitude;
- duration;
- rate of onset;
- location;
- frequency or spectrum;
- correlation structure;
- reversibility;
- whether they target components, interfaces, boundaries, or flows.

## 16. Time horizon

Persistence is always relative to a time horizon `T` and a temporal resolution. A system may persist over one operational horizon and fail over another.

Every claim must specify whether `T` represents:

- a fixed physical duration;
- a number of cycles;
- a number of updates or generations;
- a task episode;
- a return interval;
- a statistical stopping time.

# Part IV — Coherence

## 17. General definition

**Coherence** is the degree to which declared relations among selected variables exhibit organized compatibility over a stated scale, metric, and time window.

Coherence is not a single universal scalar. Every coherence claim must identify:

1. the variables being related;
2. the relation considered coherent;
3. the spatial or organizational scale;
4. the temporal window;
5. the estimator;
6. the null model;
7. the uncertainty.

The generic notation is:

```latex
\[
r_{a,m,W}(t)
\in
[0,1]
\tag{CUC-15}
\]
```

where `a` identifies scale or aggregation, `m` identifies the metric, and `W` identifies the observation window.

## 18. Phase coherence

For a population of `N` oscillatory units with phases `\theta_j(t)`, the standard global phase-order parameter is:

```latex
\[
r_G(t)
=
\left|
\frac{1}{N}
\sum_{j=1}^{N}
e^{i\theta_j(t)}
\right|
\tag{CUC-16}
\]
```

This is a useful observable for phase models. It is not a universal definition of coherence outside that regime.

For module `a` containing `N_a` units, local phase coherence may be measured as:

```latex
\[
r_a(t)
=
\left|
\frac{1}{N_a}
\sum_{j\in a}
e^{i\theta_j(t)}
\right|
\tag{CUC-17}
\]
```

The weighted mean local coherence is:

```latex
\[
r_L(t)
=
\sum_{a=1}^{K}
\frac{N_a}{N}
r_a(t)
\tag{CUC-18}
\]
```

The difference between local and global coherence can reveal modular coordination that a single global order parameter hides:

```latex
\[
\Delta_{LG}(t)
=
r_L(t)-r_G(t)
\tag{CUC-19}
\]
```

A large positive `\Delta_{LG}` may indicate coherent modules that are mutually misaligned rather than globally disordered components.

## 19. Interface coherence

For an interface edge set `E_I`, phase-interface coherence may be measured by:

```latex
\[
r_I(t)
=
\left|
\frac{1}{|E_I|}
\sum_{(i,j)\in E_I}
e^{i(\theta_i(t)-\theta_j(t))}
\right|
\tag{CUC-20}
\]
```

The corresponding interface mismatch is:

```latex
\[
M_I(t)
=
1-r_I(t)
\tag{CUC-21}
\]
```

Other domains must replace phase difference with an appropriate compatibility relation. Interface coherence may concern protocol agreement, impedance matching, semantic alignment, timing compatibility, resource exchange, causal handoff, or boundary selectivity.

## 20. Coherence matrix

When one scalar is inadequate, the framework should report a coherence profile or matrix:

```latex
\[
\mathbf{R}(t)
=
\begin{bmatrix}
r_{\mathrm{local}} & r_{\mathrm{interface}} & r_{\mathrm{global}} \\
r_{\mathrm{temporal}} & r_{\mathrm{functional}} & r_{\mathrm{causal}}
\end{bmatrix}
\tag{CUC-22}
\]
```

The entries need not share the same estimator, but their normalization and interpretation must be declared.

## 20.1 Non-phase coherence

Phase coherence is only one CUC instantiation. When the system lacks a defensible phase variable, another relation must be defined rather than relabeling similarity as phase.

Possible families include:

- **functional coherence:** coordinated preservation of a declared input-output function;
- **informational coherence:** stable dependence, coding compatibility, or predictive information;
- **causal coherence:** consistency of intervention-response relations;
- **topological coherence:** persistence of selected connectivity or shape invariants;
- **semantic coherence:** compatibility among meanings under an explicit representation and evaluator;
- **temporal coherence:** predictable relational structure across time without periodic phase;
- **protocol coherence:** successful interoperable exchange under declared rules.

A normalized functional-coherence example is:

```latex
\[
r_F(t)
=
\max
\left\{
0,
1-
\frac{
d_Y\!\left(y(t),y_\star(t)\right)
}{
d_{Y,\max}
}
\right\}
\tag{CUC-22A}
\]
```

where `y(t)` is observed system output and `y_\star(t)` is the acceptable output relation. The scale `d_{Y,\max}` must be declared.

A normalized information-dependence measure may be written:

```latex
\[
r_{\mathrm{MI}}(X,Y)
=
\frac{
I(X;Y)
}{
\sqrt{H(X)H(Y)}
}
\tag{CUC-22B}
\]
```

when both entropies are positive and the estimator’s bias is addressed. Mutual information measures statistical dependence; it does not by itself establish organization, direction, causality, or value.

For a task-based system, intervention-response coherence may be evaluated by stability of a response map:

```latex
\[
r_{\mathrm{causal}}
=
1-
\frac{
\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\mathcal{A}}
\left[
d_Y
\left(
Y_a,
\widehat{Y}_a
\right)
\right]
}{
d_{Y,\max}
}
\tag{CUC-22C}
\]
```

where `Y_a` is the observed response under intervention `a` and `\widehat{Y}_a` is the response predicted by the declared organizational model.

These examples are templates, not universally interchangeable metrics.

## 21. False coherence

CUC recognizes several forms of apparent coherence that may not support persistence:

| Type | Description | Diagnostic |
|---|---|---|
| Forced alignment | Components align only while an external driver dominates | Remove or vary the driver |
| Brittle synchrony | High order collapses under small perturbation | Perturbation-response test |
| Frozen order | Low variation is mistaken for adaptive organization | Challenge with changing conditions |
| Local lock-in | A module is coherent but incompatible with the whole | Compare local, interface, and global measures |
| Common-mode failure | Components move together toward failure | Track viability and organization independently |
| Metric coherence | The chosen statistic rises while relevant function degrades | Use competing metrics and task outcomes |
| Coercive coherence | Alignment is achieved by suppressing necessary diversity or agency | Report control burden, exclusions, and ethical costs |
| Delayed fracture | Short-window coherence conceals accumulating stress | Extend horizon and measure latent variables |

No increase in coherence should be interpreted as beneficial without simultaneous viability, organization, robustness, and value analysis.

# Part V — Fource: local coherence generation

## 22. Position of Fource

**Fource** is the proposed local, amplitude-weighted, phase-error-limited interaction functional that may generate or reinforce coherence in an appropriate dynamical model.

Fource is not asserted here to be a new fundamental physical force. It is a model primitive and sub-theorem candidate. Its validity depends on the system possessing meaningful amplitudes, phases or relational offsets, couplings, and dynamics through which the functional can act.

CUC and Fource occupy different levels:

> **Fource concerns how local coherence may be generated. CUC concerns whether declared organization persists once coherent and incoherent processes act under constraint.**

## 23. Historical mnemonic

The earliest Fource mnemonic was:

```latex
\[
F
=
\frac{A_1A_2}{\Delta\phi}
\tag{HIST-3}
\]
```

This expressed the intuition that larger amplitudes and smaller phase error strengthen relational influence. It was singular at zero phase difference, ambiguous under phase wrapping, and dimensionally incomplete. It is retained only as historical provenance.

## 24. Wrapped phase difference

For oscillatory variables, the signed wrapped phase difference is:

```latex
\[
\Delta\theta_{ij}
=
\operatorname{atan2}
\left(
\sin(\theta_i-\theta_j),
\cos(\theta_i-\theta_j)
\right)
\in(-\pi,\pi]
\tag{FOU-1}
\]
```

## 25. Regularized Fource functionals

Two useful model forms are retained.

The absolute-error form is:

```latex
\[
\Phi_{ij}^{(1)}
=
\frac{
A_iA_j
}{
|\Delta\theta_{ij}|+\varepsilon_\theta
}
\tag{FOU-2}
\]
```

The smooth quadratic-error form is:

```latex
\[
\Phi_{ij}^{(2)}
=
\frac{
A_iA_j
}{
(\Delta\theta_{ij})^2+\varepsilon_\theta
}
\tag{FOU-3}
\]
```

where `\varepsilon_\theta>0` is a declared regularization parameter. The second form is generally easier to differentiate and optimize. Neither form becomes causal until embedded in a dynamical equation.

If amplitudes carry units, a reference amplitude should be used to produce a dimensionless functional:

```latex
\[
\widetilde{\Phi}_{ij}
=
\frac{
(A_i/A_0)(A_j/A_0)
}{
(\Delta\theta_{ij})^2+\varepsilon_\theta
}
\tag{FOU-4}
\]
```

## 26. Aggregate Fource

For weighted interactions `w_{ij}\geq0`, normalized aggregate Fource is:

```latex
\[
\bar{\Phi}(t)
=
\frac{
\sum_{i<j}w_{ij}\Phi_{ij}(t)
}{
\sum_{i<j}w_{ij}
}
\tag{FOU-5}
\]
```

provided the total weight is nonzero.

Module-specific and interface-specific aggregates should be reported when topology is heterogeneous:

```latex
\[
\bar{\Phi}_{a}(t)
=
\frac{
\sum_{(i,j)\in E_a}w_{ij}\Phi_{ij}(t)
}{
\sum_{(i,j)\in E_a}w_{ij}
}
\tag{FOU-6}
\]
```

```latex
\[
\bar{\Phi}_{I}(t)
=
\frac{
\sum_{(i,j)\in E_I}w_{ij}\Phi_{ij}(t)
}{
\sum_{(i,j)\in E_I}w_{ij}
}
\tag{FOU-7}
\]
```

## 27. Dynamical embedding

A generic phase-dynamical embedding is:

```latex
\[
\dot{\theta}_i
=
\omega_i
+
\kappa
\sum_{j=1}^{N}
a_{ij}
g\!\left(\widetilde{\Phi}_{ij}\right)
\sin(\theta_j-\theta_i)
+
u_i(t)
+
\xi_i(t)
\tag{FOU-8}
\]
```

where:

- `\omega_i` is intrinsic frequency;
- `a_{ij}` is adjacency or coupling availability;
- `\kappa` is coupling gain;
- `g` bounds or transforms the Fource functional;
- `u_i(t)` is a modeled control or forcing term;
- `\xi_i(t)` is disturbance or noise.

The function `g` should normally be bounded to prevent an arbitrary regularization choice from producing unbounded effective coupling. One possible model is:

```latex
\[
g(z)
=
\frac{z}{1+z}
\tag{FOU-9}
\]
```

This is an example, not a canonical requirement.

## 28. Reduced coherence closure

A reduced descriptive closure previously associated with Fource is:

```latex
\[
\widehat{r}
=
\frac{
\bar{\Phi}
}{
\bar{\Phi}
+
\sigma_\omega
+
\eta
}
\tag{FOU-10}
\]
```

where `\sigma_\omega` represents intrinsic heterogeneity and `\eta` represents effective noise or decohering influence.

Within this reduced closure, the precise threshold implication is:

```latex
\[
\bar{\Phi}
>
\sigma_\omega+\eta
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\widehat{r}
>
\frac{1}{2}
\tag{FOU-11}
\]
```

The weaker statement that this inequality merely implies positive coherence is mathematically uninformative because the closure is already positive whenever `\bar{\Phi}>0`. Equation FOU-11 is a property of the reduced formula, not yet a universal synchronization theorem.

## 29. Fource sub-theorem candidate

The following statement is proposed as the formal Fource sub-theorem candidate:

> In a declared interaction model with bounded coupling, meaningful phase variables, nonzero interaction topology, finite heterogeneity, and finite disturbance, increasing amplitude-weighted phase compatibility can increase the probability or degree of coherent organization, provided the induced coupling does not destabilize viability or interfaces.

This candidate is deliberately conditional. It can fail under delay, frustration, antagonistic coupling, topology change, amplitude death, overload, common-mode instability, or a mismatch between the selected coherence metric and the organization that matters.

# Part VI — Persistence

## 30. Organizational survival indicator

The organizational survival indicator is:

```latex
\[
I_O(t)
=
\mathbf{1}
\left\{
d_O
\left(
\mathcal{O}(x(t)),
O_0
\right)
\leq
\varepsilon_O
\right\}
\tag{CUC-23}
\]
```

The joint organizational-viability indicator is:

```latex
\[
I_S(t)
=
I_O(t)I_V(t)
\tag{CUC-24}
\]
```

## 31. Continuous survival

For perturbation protocol `\Pi`, the primary CUC persistence quantity is the probability that organization and viability both survive continuously over the horizon:

```latex
\[
P_T^{\mathrm{cont}}
=
\Pr_{\Pi}
\left[
I_S(t)=1
\quad
\forall t\in[0,T]
\right]
\tag{CUC-25}
\]
```

Equivalently, define the failure time:

```latex
\[
\tau_{\mathrm{fail}}
=
\inf
\left\{
t\geq0:
I_S(t)=0
\right\}
\tag{CUC-26}
\]
```

Then the survival function is:

```latex
\[
S(T)
=
\Pr_{\Pi}
\left[
\tau_{\mathrm{fail}}>T
\right]
\tag{CUC-27}
\]
```

Within a declared model, `P_T^{\mathrm{cont}}` and `S(T)` coincide.

## 32. Occupancy persistence

Some systems may leave the viable organizational region briefly and recover. For those systems, occupancy persistence is:

```latex
\[
P_T^{\mathrm{occ}}
=
\frac{1}{T}
\int_0^T
I_S(t)
\,dt
\tag{CUC-28}
\]
```

Continuous survival and occupancy persistence answer different questions and must not be conflated.

## 33. Grace-period persistence

If temporary excursions are permitted, the maximum allowed continuous excursion duration `\tau_g` must be specified. A grace-period survival rule may be defined by:

```latex
\[
I_G
=
\mathbf{1}
\left\{
\sup_k
\left(
t_k^{\mathrm{return}}
-
t_k^{\mathrm{exit}}
\right)
\leq
\tau_g
\right\}
\tag{CUC-29}
\]
```

This is useful for self-repairing, homeostatic, and fault-tolerant systems.

## 34. Persistence is conditional

Every persistence quantity is conditional on at least:

- the selected boundary;
- the organization map;
- viability requirements;
- initial-condition distribution;
- perturbation protocol;
- environmental regime;
- observation horizon;
- measurement resolution.

A more explicit notation is:

```latex
\[
P_T
=
P_T
\left(
\mathcal{B},
\mathcal{O},
\mathcal{V},
\mathcal{M},
\Pi,
\mathcal{E},
\mathcal{Q}
\right)
\tag{CUC-30}
\]
```

The shorter notation may be used only after these dependencies have been declared.

## 35. Reduced CUC propensity score

For compact modeling, a dimensionless **persistence-support propensity** may be defined as:

```latex
\[
Q_{\mathrm{CUC}}(t)
=
K(t)
\,
\mathcal{R}
\left(
\bar{\Phi}(t),
\sigma_\omega(t),
\eta(t)
\right)
\,
\chi_{\mathcal{M}}(t)
\,
\psi_J(t)
\,
\frac{
e^{-D(t)}
}{
1+\lambda_{\mathrm{bad}}(t)
}
\tag{CUC-31}
\]
```

where:

- `K(t)` is a normalized organizational capacity or integrity factor;
- `\mathcal{R}` maps local interaction conditions into a coherence estimate;
- `\chi_{\mathcal{M}}` is constraint fitness;
- `\psi_J` is throughput adequacy;
- `D(t)` is the Darkness Functional;
- `\lambda_{\mathrm{bad}}` is excess or organization-degrading loss.

Equation CUC-31 is a **reduced model**, not the definition of persistence and not a universal law. Its factors must be normalized and independently operationalized. If the terms cannot be estimated without using the survival outcome itself, the model is circular and cannot serve as an independent predictor.

## 36. Mapping propensity to survival

Because `Q_{\mathrm{CUC}}` is not automatically a probability, a statistical observation model is required. One candidate relates propensity to failure hazard:

```latex
\[
h(t)
=
h_0(t)
\exp
\left[
-\beta_Q
Q_{\mathrm{CUC}}(t)
\right]
\tag{CUC-32}
\]
```

with survival prediction:

```latex
\[
\widehat{S}(T)
=
\exp
\left[
-
\int_0^T
h(t)
\,dt
\right]
\tag{CUC-33}
\]
```

The baseline hazard `h_0`, coefficient `\beta_Q`, and functional form must be estimated and compared with simpler alternatives. A logistic, proportional-hazards, state-space, or mechanistic model may be more suitable in a given domain.

## 37. Persistence profile

No single statistic is sufficient for all systems. A recommended persistence profile is:

```latex
\[
\mathbf{P}_T
=
\left(
S(T),
P_T^{\mathrm{occ}},
\mathbb{E}[\tau_{\mathrm{fail}}],
\tau_{\mathrm{return}},
\Delta O_{\max},
C_{\mathrm{repair}}
\right)
\tag{CUC-34}
\]
```

where `\Delta O_{\max}` is maximum organizational displacement and `C_{\mathrm{repair}}` is the cost of recovery under a declared accounting scheme.

## 37.1 Robustness

**Robustness** is the ability to remain within the accepted organizational and viability region without requiring substantial reconfiguration under a declared perturbation class.

For perturbations with magnitude no greater than `\delta`, a robustness profile may be defined as:

```latex
\[
B_T(\delta)
=
\Pr
\left[
\tau_{\mathrm{fail}}>T
\mid
\|p\|\leq\delta
\right]
\tag{CUC-34A}
\]
```

The perturbation norm, distribution, and target must be specified. Robustness to one perturbation family does not imply robustness to another.

## 37.2 Resilience

**Resilience** is the ability to survive or temporarily depart from an operating region and then recover acceptable organization and viability within declared time and cost bounds.

A resilience probability may be defined as:

```latex
\[
R_T
=
\Pr
\left[
\tau_{\mathrm{return}}
\leq
\tau_{\max}
\land
C_{\mathrm{repair}}
\leq
C_{\max}
\land
\min_{t\in[0,t_p+\tau_{\mathrm{return}}]}
I_V(t)
=1
\right]
\tag{CUC-34B}
\]
```

This form requires continuous viability. A grace-period variant may instead use Equation CUC-29 and must state the allowed excursion duration.

## 37.3 Adaptation and transformation

**Adaptation** changes parameters, policies, components, or constraints in order to preserve the declared organization. **Transformation** changes one organizational level while preserving a higher-order invariant, purpose, lineage, or value commitment.

Neither term is synonymous with persistence. They describe pathways through which persistence at one level may be achieved or deliberately relinquished at another.

# Part VII — Darkness and degradation

## 38. Position of the Darkness Functional

The **Darkness Functional** is a complementary diagnostic of conditions that obstruct, fragment, distort, or conceal coherence and persistence. It is not moral darkness and must not be interpreted as a value judgment about a person, population, or system.

Darkness is modeled as nonnegative:

```latex
\[
D(t)
\geq
0
\tag{DAR-1}
\]
```

## 39. Baseline Darkness model

A normalized baseline form is:

```latex
\[
D(t)
=
\alpha
\left(1-r_G(t)\right)
+
\beta N(t)
+
\gamma B_F(t)
+
\delta M_I(t)
\tag{DAR-2}
\]
```

where:

- `N(t)` is normalized noise, uncertainty, or unresolved disturbance;
- `B_F(t)` is boundary-failure severity;
- `M_I(t)` is interface mismatch;
- `\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta` are nonnegative weights.

The weights must be estimated, justified, or used in sensitivity analysis. They may not be selected solely to reproduce a desired conclusion.

## 40. Extended Darkness model

Where supported, the diagnostic may include delay, hidden stress, and adversarial distortion:

```latex
\[
D_{\mathrm{ext}}(t)
=
D(t)
+
\zeta L_\tau(t)
+
\rho H_S(t)
+
\upsilon A_D(t)
\tag{DAR-3}
\]
```

where `L_\tau` is damaging latency, `H_S` is hidden accumulated stress, and `A_D` is adversarial or systematic distortion.

## 41. Darkness attenuation

The attenuation factor used in a reduced model is:

```latex
\[
a_D(t)
=
e^{-D(t)}
\in
(0,1]
\tag{DAR-4}
\]
```

This exponential form is convenient but not canonical. It must be compared with threshold, saturating, and interaction models. In particular, boundary failure and interface mismatch may interact nonlinearly rather than add independently.

## 42. Light and Darkness

The Light Functional and Light/Dark Functional Calculus remain adjacent branches rather than silently becoming foundational CUC primitives. A future canon may define **expressed coherence** as the portion of organization that becomes observable, transmissible, or usable under actual boundary and throughput conditions. Until that definition is operationally fixed and tested, Light remains a derived research module.

# Part VIII — Return and the Orbital Coherence Principle

## 43. Return

**Return** is recovery to an acceptable organizational equivalence class after displacement. It does not require return to an identical microscopic state.

Given a perturbation ending at `t_p`, the first return time is:

```latex
\[
\tau_{\mathrm{return}}
=
\inf
\left\{
\tau>0:
d_O
\left(
\mathcal{O}(x(t_p+\tau)),
O_0
\right)
\leq
\varepsilon_R
\right\}
\tag{OCP-1}
\]
```

Return must be evaluated together with viability. A trajectory that revisits a measured coordinate after irreversible organizational failure is not a successful return.

## 44. Orbital Coherence Principle

The **Orbital Coherence Principle**, abbreviated **OCP**, is a validation principle:

> **An orbit is coherent when bounded relational state returns under constraint; metaphorical orbit cannot substitute for dynamical proof.**

Its gate is:

```latex
\[
OC
=
\mathrm{BOUNDED}
\land
\mathrm{RETURN}
\land
\mathrm{PHASE}
\land
\mathrm{SURVIVAL}
\land
\mathrm{MODEL}
\tag{OCP-2}
\]
```

Each term is a required gate:

- **BOUNDED:** the relevant trajectory or relational state remains within declared bounds;
- **RETURN:** the organization or relational state returns within tolerance;
- **PHASE:** any phase claim is supported by an actual phase variable or justified analogue;
- **SURVIVAL:** return persists under a declared perturbation regime;
- **MODEL:** the dynamical model is adequate relative to alternatives and residual diagnostics.

The associated return phrase is:

> **Fall without collapse. Move without escape.**

## 45. Boundedness and recurrence tests

A simple boundedness condition is:

```latex
\[
\sup_{t\in[0,T]}
d_X
\left(
x(t),
x_{\mathrm{ref}}
\right)
<
B
\tag{OCP-3}
\]
```

A relational recurrence condition is:

```latex
\[
d_R
\left(
R(x(t+\tau)),
R(x(t))
\right)
\leq
\varepsilon_R
\tag{OCP-4}
\]
```

for a declared relation map `R`, lag `\tau`, and tolerance `\varepsilon_R`.

OCP is not a replacement for CUC. It is one protocol for testing return-like persistence in systems where bounded relational recurrence is meaningful.

# Part IX — Foundational commitments

## 46. Commitment A: identity is organizational

CUC treats persistence as preservation of a declared organization, not preservation of every component or microscopic coordinate. Components may be replaced, roles may rotate, phases may advance, and internal trajectories may vary while organization remains within its accepted equivalence class.

This commitment prevents static sameness from becoming the only form of persistence. It also creates an obligation: the researcher must declare which transformations preserve identity and which constitute failure.

## 47. Commitment B: constraints define possibility

Constraints are part of the architecture of possible behavior. They are not treated only as external opposition. A channel constrains a flow and makes directed flow possible; a protocol forbids many messages and permits reliable exchange; an orbit is possible because motion is bounded by a dynamical relation.

The research question is therefore not merely, “How much constraint exists?” It is:

> **How does the geometry of admissible states and transitions affect organization, viability, and return?**

## 48. Commitment C: coherence is relational and scale-dependent

Coherence belongs to specified relations among specified variables. A system cannot be called coherent without naming the scale and metric. Local coherence, interface coherence, global coherence, temporal coherence, functional coherence, and causal coherence may diverge.

## 49. Commitment D: generation and persistence are distinct

Mechanisms that generate alignment may not preserve it. Mechanisms that preserve an organization may not have generated it. Fource belongs to the generation layer; CUC belongs to the persistence layer; Darkness describes selected degradation pathways; OCP tests bounded return.

## 50. Commitment E: persistence is horizon-relative

There is no horizon-free persistence result. Every statement is indexed by an interval, number of trials, recurrence count, or stopping rule. Short-term survival may coexist with long-term fragility.

## 51. Commitment F: viability and coherence are independent observables

High coherence does not imply viability, and viability does not require maximal coherence. This independence must be maintained in data collection and model construction.

## 52. Commitment G: heterogeneity may be functional

Variation, phase offsets, modular specialization, asynchronous operation, and controlled disagreement can support persistence. CUC does not equate perfect uniformity with ideal organization.

## 53. Commitment H: flow has an operating window

Insufficient flow can starve a structure; excessive flow can overload or dissolve it. Throughput must be evaluated relative to capacity, timing, routing, and boundary quality.

## 54. Commitment I: coherence is ethically neutral

Coherent systems can nurture, exploit, heal, deceive, cooperate, dominate, or destroy. CUC may describe organization and persistence, but it cannot supply an ethical objective without an independent value framework.

## 55. Commitment J: every model is bounded

CUC claims are valid only within declared assumptions, scales, domains, measurement procedures, and uncertainty. Cross-domain transfer requires a new mapping and new evidence.

## 56. Commitment K: falsification outranks narrative rescue

When a preregistered prediction fails, the failure must be recorded. A revised model may be proposed, but it receives a new version and may not retroactively replace the tested claim.

## 57. Commitment L: simpler alternatives must be compared

A CUC model must be compared with reasonable baselines. If raw coupling, energy balance, network connectivity, ordinary control theory, or a simpler survival model explains the outcome equally well, the added CUC terms have not yet demonstrated incremental value.

# Part X — Multiscale organization and interfaces

## 58. Levels of analysis

A mature CUC model should distinguish at least four levels when the domain permits:

1. **Component level:** state and behavior of individual units.
2. **Module level:** organization within functional or topological groups.
3. **Interface level:** compatibility and exchange across groups or boundaries.
4. **System level:** global organization, viability, and persistence.

Environmental feedback may be added as a fifth level rather than absorbed into unexplained noise.

## 59. Multiscale state

A hierarchical state description may be written:

```latex
\[
x(t)
=
\left(
x^{(0)}(t),
x^{(1)}(t),
\ldots,
x^{(L)}(t)
\right)
\tag{CUC-35}
\]
```

where levels need not be independent. A coarse-graining map connects adjacent levels:

```latex
\[
x^{(\ell+1)}(t)
=
\Gamma_\ell
\left(
x^{(\ell)}(t)
\right)
\tag{CUC-36}
\]
```

A persistence claim at level `\ell+1` should state how much microscopic variation at level `\ell` is compatible with preserved higher-level organization.

## 60. Interface bottleneck hypothesis

When modules are internally coherent but mutually incompatible, persistence may be controlled more strongly by interface quality than by average local coherence.

A simple candidate model is:

```latex
\[
Q_{\mathrm{interface}}
=
r_L
\,
r_I
\tag{CUC-37}
\]
```

An alternative bottleneck model is:

```latex
\[
Q_{\mathrm{bottleneck}}
=
\min
\left\{
r_1,
r_2,
\ldots,
r_K,
r_I
\right\}
\tag{CUC-38}
\]
```

Neither form is canonical. They represent competing hypotheses to be evaluated against arithmetic-mean, geometric-mean, and network-flow alternatives.

## 61. Coherence gradients

Sharp differences in relational organization across a boundary may predict stress or failure even when each side appears coherent in isolation. A generic coherence-gradient diagnostic is:

```latex
\[
G_R(t)
=
\sum_{(a,b)\in E_M}
w_{ab}
\left|
r_a(t)-r_b(t)
\right|
\tag{CUC-39}
\]
```

This diagnostic should not replace direct interface measurement when such measurement is available.

## 62. Compositional persistence

The persistence of a whole cannot generally be inferred by multiplying the persistence of its components. Dependencies, common-mode shocks, redundant pathways, repair, and load redistribution matter.

For component failure times `\tau_i`, the system failure time is determined by its architecture:

```latex
\[
\tau_{\mathrm{sys}}
=
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{arch}}
\left(
\tau_1,
\tau_2,
\ldots,
\tau_N
\right)
\tag{CUC-40}
\]
```

The architecture functional `\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{arch}}` must represent series, parallel, threshold, network, graceful-degradation, or repairable structure as appropriate.

# Part XI — Constraint architecture

## 63. Enabling and restrictive effects

The same constraint may be enabling at one scale and restrictive at another. A narrow interface may reduce noise while limiting throughput. A protocol may improve interoperability while suppressing local adaptation. A stable orbit may preserve bounded motion while excluding escape.

CUC therefore reports a constraint-effect vector rather than assuming a single sign:

```latex
\[
\mathbf{m}(t)
=
\left(
m_{\mathrm{stability}},
m_{\mathrm{adaptation}},
m_{\mathrm{throughput}},
m_{\mathrm{diversity}},
m_{\mathrm{repair}},
m_{\mathrm{cost}}
\right)
\tag{CUC-41}
\]
```

## 64. Constraint window hypothesis

Many CUC systems are expected to exhibit a viable constraint window: too little constraint permits diffusion or incoherence, while too much constraint creates rigidity, overload, or inability to adapt.

A generic unimodal candidate is:

```latex
\[
\psi_M(m)
=
\exp
\left[
-
\frac{
(m-m_\star)^2
}{
2s_M^2
}
\right]
\tag{CUC-42}
\]
```

This equation is illustrative. The actual relation may be monotonic, thresholded, multi-peaked, hysteretic, or discontinuous.

## 65. Constraint adaptation

Some systems alter their own admissible transition structure. Let constraint parameters be `m(t)`. Adaptive constraint dynamics may be represented by:

```latex
\[
\dot{m}(t)
=
g
\left(
m(t),
x(t),
e(t),
\nabla \widehat{P}(t)
\right)
\tag{CUC-43}
\]
```

This introduces second-order persistence: not only surviving within constraints, but modifying constraints while preserving organization. Such adaptation can improve resilience or produce runaway self-locking and must be tested under changing environments.

## 66. Constraint debt

A system may maintain short-term order by accumulating deferred fragility. **Constraint debt** is the hidden future cost created when immediate stability is achieved by reducing adaptability, consuming repair capacity, concentrating load, or suppressing necessary variation.

A generic cumulative diagnostic is:

```latex
\[
C_D(T)
=
\int_0^T
\left[
c_{\mathrm{rigid}}(t)
+
c_{\mathrm{repair}}(t)
+
c_{\mathrm{latent}}(t)
\right]
dt
\tag{CUC-44}
\]
```

Constraint debt is a research construct, not yet a canonical measurable quantity. Its purpose is to prevent short-horizon coherence from being mistaken for long-horizon persistence.

# Part XII — Core hypotheses and falsifiers

## 67. Hypothesis registry rule

Every hypothesis must include:

- a stable identifier;
- a precise claim;
- its model class and domain;
- independent and dependent variables;
- predicted direction or functional form;
- a null or rival model;
- a falsification condition;
- a preregistered analysis when feasible;
- evidence level and version.

## 68. CUC-H1: local compatibility hypothesis

**Claim:** Within an appropriate phase-coupled model and a stable operating regime, increased amplitude-weighted phase compatibility predicts increased subsequent phase coherence.

**Operational prediction:** Lagged `\bar{\Phi}(t)` improves prediction of `r_G(t+\Delta t)` beyond amplitude, phase difference, coupling strength, and topology entered separately.

**Falsifier:** Across the declared regime, `\bar{\Phi}` provides no out-of-sample predictive gain or its effect reverses reproducibly after confounds are controlled.

## 69. CUC-H2: coherence-survival hypothesis

**Claim:** Coherence measures selected for their relevance to organization predict survival under perturbation after controlling for energy balance, topology, and constraint fitness.

**Operational prediction:** A model containing preregistered local, interface, or global coherence measures predicts `S(T)` better than matched models without them.

**Falsifier:** Coherence terms do not improve calibrated out-of-sample survival prediction or are fully replaced by simpler variables.

## 70. CUC-H3: constraint-fitness hypothesis

**Claim:** Constraint fitness predicts persistence more reliably than raw constraint magnitude.

**Operational prediction:** Models using `\chi_{\mathcal{M}}` or an independently estimated proxy outperform models using constraint intensity alone across environments.

**Falsifier:** Raw magnitude consistently performs as well or better, or the fitness estimator cannot be measured without embedding the outcome.

## 71. CUC-H4: throughput-window hypothesis

**Claim:** For throughput-dependent systems, persistence is maximized within an operating region rather than at maximal throughput.

**Operational prediction:** Survival or organization exhibits a preregistered non-monotonic relationship with independently manipulated throughput.

**Falsifier:** Within the claimed regime, the relation is reliably monotonic or throughput has no effect after accounting for relevant variables.

## 72. CUC-H5: interface-bottleneck hypothesis

**Claim:** In modular systems, interface coherence or compatibility explains failures that are not predicted by mean local coherence.

**Operational prediction:** Interface metrics improve failure localization and survival prediction when module-level metrics remain high.

**Falsifier:** Interface measures provide no incremental prediction and module or global measures fully account for outcomes.

## 73. CUC-H6: Darkness-hazard hypothesis

**Claim:** A preregistered Darkness Functional predicts increased failure hazard beyond any one of its component terms.

**Operational prediction:** The composite improves calibrated out-of-sample hazard prediction and retains stable weights or functional behavior across replications.

**Falsifier:** The composite is unstable, adds no predictive value, or succeeds only through post hoc weight selection.

## 74. CUC-H7: bounded-return hypothesis

**Claim:** Systems satisfying the complete OCP gate show more reliable organizational return after perturbation than systems satisfying only apparent periodicity or synchrony.

**Operational prediction:** The full gate predicts return probability, return time, and post-return survival beyond spectral periodicity alone.

**Falsifier:** The full gate has no advantage over simpler recurrence measures or fails to distinguish forced repetition from resilient return.

## 75. CUC-H8: multiscale divergence hypothesis

**Claim:** Divergence among local, interface, and global coherence is an early-warning signal for certain organizational failures.

**Operational prediction:** `\Delta_{LG}`, `M_I`, or a preregistered multiscale feature predicts failure earlier than `r_G` alone.

**Falsifier:** Multiscale divergence does not improve lead time, discrimination, or calibration across the declared class of failures.

## 76. CUC-H9: false-coherence hypothesis

**Claim:** High coherence without perturbation robustness, viability, or organizational competence constitutes a distinct and detectable regime.

**Operational prediction:** Systems matched on baseline coherence separate into persistent and nonpersistent groups under controlled challenge, using robustness and viability measures specified in advance.

**Falsifier:** Baseline coherence alone predicts challenged survival as well as the expanded profile within the claimed regime.

## 77. CUC-H10: adaptive-constraint hypothesis

**Claim:** Systems that can alter constraints within bounded governance rules persist across a broader environmental range than systems with fixed constraints, without incurring greater catastrophic-tail risk.

**Operational prediction:** Adaptive systems improve survival across regime shifts while maintaining bounded failure severity.

**Falsifier:** Adaptation offers no range benefit, or its average benefit is offset by unacceptable tail failures under the declared criterion.

## 78. Null framework

The following null positions must remain available:

- CUC variables add no predictive value beyond established domain variables.
- Fource is a reparameterization of ordinary coupling with no independent explanatory content.
- Darkness is an unstable composite of already sufficient predictors.
- constraint fitness cannot be independently operationalized;
- apparent cross-domain recurrence is metaphorical rather than structural;
- one or more CUC hypotheses hold only in narrow toy-model regimes.

The framework advances by surviving these nulls, not by excluding them rhetorically.

# Part XIII — Evidence and validation ladder

## 79. Evidence levels

CUC uses the following evidence ladder:

| Level | Name | Minimum requirement |
|---|---|---|
| E0 | Metaphorical intuition | Clear analogy, explicitly non-evidentiary |
| E1 | Formal specification | Defined variables, assumptions, dimensional or type consistency |
| E2 | Analytical or computational result | Correct derivation or reproducible simulation |
| E3 | Controlled synthetic experiment | Independent manipulation, baselines, uncertainty, held-out testing |
| E4 | Cross-model replication | Result reproduced across topologies, parameters, seeds, or model families |
| E5 | Domain observation | Operational mapping to real measurements with competing explanations |
| E6 | Prospective prediction | Preregistered prediction succeeds on unseen data or future events |
| E7 | Intervention or engineering validation | CUC-guided intervention reliably improves a declared outcome |
| E8 | Multi-domain structural replication | The same formal relation survives independent domain translations without variable laundering |

Evidence does not automatically climb because a document uses more mathematical notation. A beautifully specified E1 model remains E1 until tested.

## 80. Promotion rules

A claim may be promoted only if:

1. its variables are operationally defined;
2. its equation or procedure is reproducible;
3. its assumptions are stated;
4. plausible rival models are included;
5. uncertainty and sensitivity are reported;
6. failures and negative results are retained;
7. the evidence level is independently auditable;
8. promotion does not exceed the narrowest supporting result.

## 81. Claim-boundary discipline

Every manuscript, repository, presentation, or application should distinguish the following sentences:

- “We define…”
- “The model implies…”
- “The simulation demonstrates within this model…”
- “The data are consistent with…”
- “The result predicts…”
- “We speculate…”
- “This result does not establish…”

These are not stylistic hedges. They identify different epistemic acts.

## 82. Reproducibility requirements

A computational CUC result should include:

- a machine-readable environment;
- source code and license;
- fixed random seeds or seed policy;
- parameter files;
- exact commands;
- raw or reconstructible outputs;
- tests of core equations;
- expected figure names and paths;
- convergence or numerical-stability checks;
- a manifest linking claims to figures and code;
- known limitations.

Hard-coded machine paths, mismatched filenames, decorative plots presented as simulated outcomes, and confounded parameter manipulations block promotion beyond prototype status.

## 82.1 Identifiability

A parameter is not empirically meaningful merely because it appears in an equation. CUC studies must ask whether distinct parameter values or mechanisms produce distinguishable observations.

Let `\vartheta` denote model parameters and `p(y\mid\vartheta)` the observable-data distribution. Structural identifiability requires:

```latex
\[
p(y\mid\vartheta_1)
=
p(y\mid\vartheta_2)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\vartheta_1
=
\vartheta_2
\tag{VAL-1}
\]
```

within the declared parameter space, or a documented equivalence class when exact identification is impossible.

This is especially important for separating:

- throughput from coupling;
- constraint strength from alignment forcing;
- amplitude weighting from phase-error weighting;
- noise from unresolved deterministic heterogeneity;
- boundary failure from interface mismatch;
- coherence effects from topology and energy balance.

## 82.2 Causal claims

Predictive association does not establish that increasing coherence, Fource, constraint fitness, or throughput adequacy will improve persistence. A causal claim requires intervention, justified natural experiment, or a defensible causal model.

A generic intervention contrast is:

```latex
\[
\Delta_Y
=
\mathbb{E}
\left[
Y
\mid
\operatorname{do}(Z=z_1)
\right]
-
\mathbb{E}
\left[
Y
\mid
\operatorname{do}(Z=z_0)
\right]
\tag{VAL-2}
\]
```

The intervention variable `Z`, outcome `Y`, confounding assumptions, spillovers, and measurement process must be explicit. In dynamical systems, intervention timing and feedback may invalidate static causal contrasts; sequential or control-theoretic methods may be required.

# Part XIV — Reference experimental program

## 83. Purpose of the reference benchmark

The next empirical season of CUC should begin with one disciplined reference system rather than many loosely connected domain claims. A modular oscillator network is recommended because phase, amplitude, topology, coupling, perturbation, and return can be manipulated explicitly.

The benchmark is not intended to prove that all systems are oscillators. It is intended to create a transparent test bed for the framework’s internal claims.

## 84. Reference dynamics

A baseline modular phase model is:

```latex
\[
d\theta_i
=
\left[
\omega_i
+
\sum_{j=1}^{N}
K_{ij}
\sin
\left(
\theta_j-\theta_i-\alpha_{ij}
\right)
\right]dt
+
\sigma_i\,dW_i(t)
\tag{EXP-1}
\]
```

where `K_{ij}`, `\alpha_{ij}`, topology, noise, and frequency distributions are independently controlled.

A Fource-augmented comparison model is:

```latex
\[
d\theta_i
=
\left[
\omega_i
+
\kappa
\sum_{j=1}^{N}
a_{ij}
g\!\left(\widetilde{\Phi}_{ij}\right)
\sin
\left(
\theta_j-\theta_i-\alpha_{ij}
\right)
\right]dt
+
\sigma_i\,dW_i(t)
\tag{EXP-2}
\]
```

The ordinary and Fource-augmented models must be compared using equal parameter budgets or penalized model-selection criteria.

## 85. Independent variables

The benchmark must manipulate independently:

- ordinary coupling gain;
- Fource transformation and regularization;
- within-module connectivity;
- between-module connectivity;
- constraint strength and constraint topology;
- throughput or resource availability;
- noise intensity and correlation;
- intrinsic-frequency heterogeneity;
- delay;
- dissipation or damping where amplitude dynamics are included;
- perturbation amplitude, duration, and target;
- boundary leakage or interface error.

Throughput must not be implemented solely by multiplying coupling if the goal is to distinguish throughput from coupling. Constraint must not be defined as an alignment force if the hypothesis being tested is that constraint produces alignment.

## 86. Outcome variables

At minimum, record:

- global coherence `r_G`;
- mean local coherence `r_L`;
- interface coherence `r_I`;
- local-global divergence `\Delta_{LG}`;
- organization distance `d_O`;
- viability indicator `I_V`;
- continuous survival `S(T)`;
- occupancy persistence `P_T^{\mathrm{occ}}`;
- time to failure;
- return probability and return time;
- repair cost;
- energy or resource accounting;
- failure location and mode.

## 87. Factorial design

A minimal design crosses at least:

```latex
\[
\mathrm{Design}
=
\mathrm{Coupling}
\times
\mathrm{Constraint}
\times
\mathrm{Throughput}
\times
\mathrm{Noise}
\times
\mathrm{Topology}
\times
\mathrm{Perturbation}
\tag{EXP-3}
\]
```

The first experiment may use a fractional factorial or space-filling design, but interaction terms must be estimable for the hypotheses being claimed.

## 88. Baseline models

Required comparisons should include:

1. uncoupled units;
2. ordinary Kuramoto-style coupling;
3. amplitude-only weighted coupling;
4. phase-error-only coupling;
5. Fource-augmented coupling;
6. a model using only topology and energy balance;
7. a flexible statistical predictor with no CUC structure;
8. ablations removing each Darkness term;
9. a global-only coherence model;
10. a multiscale coherence model.

## 89. Analysis plan

The benchmark should report:

- effect sizes and uncertainty, not only significance;
- calibration of survival probabilities;
- held-out prediction across parameter regions;
- sensitivity to regularization `\varepsilon_\theta`;
- sensitivity to organization tolerance `\varepsilon_O`;
- robustness across random seeds;
- phase diagrams with uncertainty;
- failure-mode distributions;
- model complexity penalties;
- identifiability diagnostics;
- residual structure;
- negative and null results.

## 90. Minimum success criterion

CUC v0.2 earns a stronger evidentiary status in the reference benchmark only if at least one preregistered CUC variable provides reproducible, out-of-sample predictive or intervention value beyond appropriate ordinary dynamical and statistical baselines.

If it does not, the framework remains conceptually useful but has not yet demonstrated distinctive empirical power.

# Part XV — Domain translation protocol

## 91. Required mapping

Every domain application must complete the following table before using CUC as an explanatory framework.

| Field | Required declaration |
|---|---|
| System | What exactly is inside the analysis? |
| Environment | Which influences remain outside? |
| Boundary | How is inclusion and exchange determined? |
| State | What variables describe the system? |
| Organization | What relations or functions define identity? |
| Equivalence | Which changes preserve identity? |
| Viability | What conditions permit continued operation? |
| Constraints | Which states or transitions are restricted or enabled? |
| Coherence | Which relation is measured, at what scale, by what estimator? |
| Fource analogue | Is there a real local interaction mechanism, or only metaphor? |
| Throughput | What flows, in what units, through which boundary? |
| Darkness terms | Which degradation variables are independently measured? |
| Perturbation | What challenge is applied or observed? |
| Horizon | Over what duration or number of cycles? |
| Persistence | What counts as survival? |
| Return | What counts as recovery, and within what tolerance? |
| Rival models | What established explanations compete? |
| Falsifier | What result would count against the mapping? |
| Evidence | What level on the CUC ladder is actually achieved? |

## 92. Translation test

A domain mapping passes the minimum translation test only if:

- at least one variable is measurable independently of the outcome;
- the organization map is not defined only after seeing what survived;
- “constraint” is more specific than difficulty or limitation;
- “coherence” is more specific than goodness, harmony, or success;
- the perturbation and horizon are explicit;
- a rival model could in principle win;
- failure of the CUC prediction would be reportable.

## 93. No variable laundering

The same word may refer to different quantities in different domains. Cross-domain work must not treat linguistic identity as mathematical identity.

For example:

- neural phase coherence is not automatically organizational agreement;
- social consensus is not automatically dynamical synchrony;
- energy flow is not automatically information throughput;
- legal constraint is not automatically a force term;
- cosmological boundary entropy is not automatically system-level Darkness.

Cross-domain equations require explicit homomorphisms, dimensional transformations, or justified abstract equivalences.

## 94. Application evidence does not flow backward automatically

A successful application can support the specific formal relation it instantiates. It does not validate every branch of CUC, and a compelling metaphor in one domain does not confirm a mechanism in another.

Evidence should flow through a claim graph, not through association with the CUC name.

# Part XVII — Ethics, safety, and governance

## 99. Coherence is not goodness

CUC must never equate persistence or coordination with moral worth. Pathological organizations can be coherent. Harmful institutions can be persistent. Deception can be internally consistent. Tumors can coordinate. Authoritarian control can suppress variance. A system can score highly on a chosen coherence metric while violating autonomy, justice, truth, ecological health, or human well-being.

## 100. Separate value functional

If an application makes normative recommendations, it must declare a value assessment independent of coherence. A generic multi-objective value vector is preferable to hiding values inside the coherence metric:

```latex
\[
\mathbf{U}(t)
=
\left(
U_{\mathrm{wellbeing}},
U_{\mathrm{autonomy}},
U_{\mathrm{justice}},
U_{\mathrm{truth}},
U_{\mathrm{ecology}},
U_{\mathrm{safety}}
\right)
\tag{ETH-1}
\]
```

The choice and weighting of these values are governance decisions, not consequences of CUC mathematics.

## 101. Human applications

When CUC is applied to people, teams, communities, or societies:

- persons must not be reduced to oscillator units without explicit justification and ethical review;
- disagreement must not automatically be labeled noise;
- dissent may be a repair signal rather than incoherence;
- imposed alignment must be distinguished from consent;
- surveillance burden and power asymmetry must be reported;
- vulnerable groups must not bear hidden constraint or failure costs;
- optimization targets must remain contestable;
- affected people should participate in defining organization and viability.

## 102. Agent and consciousness claims

Persistence of memory, identity variables, self-description, or behavioral continuity in an artificial agent does not establish subjective consciousness. CUC Habitat and Hoshi may test operational continuity, constraint handling, repair, and return. They must not promote those observations into consciousness claims without a separate theory and evidence.

## 103. Dual-use review

Methods for increasing coherence and persistence can also increase the effectiveness of harmful systems. Work that materially improves coordination, control, evasion, persuasion, or autonomous persistence should include a dual-use assessment and bounded release strategy appropriate to the risk.

## 104. Right to incoherence and transformation

Persistence is not always the desired outcome. Systems may need to dissolve, reorganize, or relinquish an obsolete identity. Human participants may have a legitimate right to exit, dissent, forget, change, or refuse alignment.

CUC engineering should therefore distinguish:

- preserving a valuable organization;
- repairing a damaged organization;
- transitioning to a new organization;
- safely terminating an organization.

## 106. Equation-source standard

Every canonical equation must be stored as raw LaTeX source, not only as a rendered image or rich-text object.

````text

=

\left[

\right]
\]
```
````

This preserves exact backslashes, subscripts, braces, spacing, and equation tags during copy and paste.

The source policy is:

1. Raw LaTeX is authoritative.
2. Rendered equations are derivative views.
3. Every equation receives a stable identifier.

## 107. Dimensional and type discipline

Before an equation is promoted to a reduced model or derived result, each term must be:

- dimensionally compatible, or explicitly normalized;
- assigned a mathematical type;
- given an allowed range;
- accompanied by an estimation procedure;
- tested at boundary values;
- checked for singularities and invariances.

Adding dimensionless normalized scores is not equivalent to adding physical quantities. A composite score must be labeled as such.

## 108. Equation-change policy

Changing a denominator, exponent, normalization, sign, range, or interpretation is a model change, not a typographic repair. The change must receive:

- a new equation version or identifier;
- a reason;
- a compatibility note;
- updated tests;
- a statement of whether prior results remain valid.

# Part XX — Failure, revision, and pruning

## 115. Failure taxonomy

CUC distinguishes at least the following failure modes:

| Failure mode | Description |
|---|---|
| Coherence loss | Relevant relations become disordered |
| Viability loss | Operational requirements are violated |
| Organizational drift | System remains active but loses defining relations |
| Boundary rupture | Exchange exceeds or bypasses regulatory capacity |
| Interface fracture | Modules remain intact but cannot coordinate |
| Throughput starvation | Required flow falls below viable range |
| Throughput overload | Flow exceeds processing or dissipation capacity |
| Constraint collapse | Enabling constraints disappear |
| Constraint lock | Constraints prevent necessary adaptation |
| Common-mode collapse | High alignment carries the whole system into failure |
| Repair exhaustion | Recovery capacity is depleted |
| Return failure | System survives but cannot recover its organization |
| Measurement failure | The chosen observable ceases to track the target construct |
| Model failure | Predictions fail despite adequate measurement and implementation |

## 116. Revision protocol

When a claim fails:

1. verify implementation and measurement integrity;
2. preserve the original result and configuration;
3. determine whether failure is numerical, operational, empirical, or conceptual;
4. update the claim ledger;
5. avoid changing the success criterion retroactively;
6. propose a revised claim with a new version;
7. test the revised claim on new conditions;
8. record whether the failure narrows a regime or removes the claim.

## 117. Pruning criteria

A branch should be pruned, archived, or quarantined when:

- its primitives cannot be operationalized;
- its claims cannot be falsified;
- it duplicates an established model without added value;
- it repeatedly depends on post hoc parameter choice;
- its equations are dimensionally or logically incoherent;
- its evidence is persistently misrepresented;
- its application risks exceed its demonstrated benefit;
- it creates registry confusion without conceptual gain.

Pruning is not erasure. Archived branches remain part of the intellectual history and may later be restored with new evidence.

## 118. Garden health review

At each minor or major release, the maintainers should review:

- roots that must remain stable;
- branches producing reproducible fruit;
- branches requiring water or formalization;
- branches crossing without clear interfaces;
- diseased notation or circular definitions;
- speculative growth requiring quarantine;
- dead branches that should be archived;
- missing evidence, ownership, and provenance.

# Part XXI — Open problems

## 119. Organization discovery

How can the organization map be learned without defining identity after observing survival? Candidate approaches include causal representation learning, persistent topology, task-equivalence classes, intervention profiles, and expert-declared functional invariants. Each risks importing bias into the definition of persistence.

## 120. Coherence metric selection

How should a model choose among phase, spectral, informational, causal, functional, semantic, and topological coherence? Metric selection should be predicted by the organization and mechanism, not chosen because it yields the desired correlation.

## 121. Constraint-fitness estimation

Equation CUC-11 is conceptually clean but often hard to compute. Research is needed on estimators based on reachable sets, viable transition sampling, control energy, constraint geometry, and counterfactual simulation.

## 122. Causal status of Fource

Does Fource provide causal or predictive content beyond ordinary coupling, amplitude weighting, and phase-error terms? This is one of the highest-priority questions because a reparameterization can be useful without constituting a distinct mechanism.

## 123. Multiscale causation

When does macro-level organization constrain micro-level dynamics in a way that is not merely descriptive? Work should distinguish causal intervention effects from coarse-grained summaries.

## 124. Adaptive coherence

What level of diversity and phase offset maximizes long-horizon persistence under changing conditions? Maximal instantaneous alignment may reduce exploration and adaptive capacity.

## 125. Darkness interactions

Are noise, boundary failure, and interface mismatch additive, multiplicative, thresholded, or state-dependent? The current baseline is a testable convenience, not an established decomposition.

## 126. Return without recurrence

Some systems return functionally while following entirely new trajectories. Research is needed on organizational return metrics that recognize repair, compensation, and reconfiguration without requiring periodic motion.

## 127. Transformative persistence

Can a system preserve identity by changing its organization? This apparent paradox requires a hierarchy of invariants: lower-level organization may transform while higher-level purpose, causal role, or lineage persists.

## 128. Ethical objective selection

Who defines the organization worth preserving, and who bears the constraints? In human applications, these questions cannot be delegated to a technical metric.

## 129. Universality and limits

Does CUC identify a genuinely recurring mathematical structure across domains, or a family resemblance best treated as a design language? Both outcomes are intellectually useful, but they require different claims.

# Appendix F — Glossary

**Adaptation:** Change to parameters, policies, components, or constraints that supports persistence of a declared organization.

**Boundary:** The operational inclusion rule and exchange interface between system and environment.

**Coherence:** Organized compatibility among declared variables, relations, scales, and time windows.

**Constraint:** A restriction or shaping condition on admissible states, transitions, or exchanges.

**Constraint fitness:** The degree to which a constraint architecture permits organization-preserving or organization-restoring transitions.

**Constraint debt:** Deferred fragility created when immediate stability consumes adaptability, repair capacity, or future operating freedom.

**Darkness:** A nonnegative diagnostic of modeled coherence- and persistence-degrading conditions.

**Equivalence class:** The set of organizational descriptions treated as preserving identity within a declared tolerance.

**Fource:** An amplitude-weighted, phase-error-limited local interaction functional proposed as a coherence-generation mechanism in appropriate models.

**Interface:** A relation or boundary across which modules, components, or systems exchange influence.

**Organization:** The relations, functions, invariants, roles, or patterns whose preservation defines identity for the study.

**Persistence:** Survival of declared organization and viability over a declared horizon and perturbation regime.

**Perturbation:** A declared displacement, disturbance, intervention, or change used to test survival or return.

**Return:** Recovery to an acceptable organizational equivalence class after displacement.

**Resilience:** Recovery of acceptable organization and viability within declared time and cost bounds after perturbation.

**Robustness:** Continued organizational and viability survival under a declared perturbation class without substantial reconfiguration.

**Structure:** A system considered through the organization whose persistence is in question.

**Throughput:** The rate of relevant energy, matter, information, work, or resource flow through or within a system.

**Throughput adequacy:** The degree to which throughput lies within the system’s viable operating region.

**Transformation:** Change at one organizational level that may preserve a higher-order invariant, purpose, lineage, or value commitment.

**Viability:** Satisfaction of declared conditions required for continued operation.

# Closing covenant

CUC began as an intuition that organization survives through a relationship between coherence and constraint. It has since grown into a framework of generation, persistence, degradation, return, boundary, throughput, repair, and transformation.

The next season is not served by adding more metaphorical branches. It is served by clearer roots, testable growth, and honest pruning.

This Charter therefore closes with five promises:

1. We will preserve the distinction between what is defined, what is derived, what is observed, and what is imagined.
2. We will make every canonical equation recoverable as exact source.
3. We will allow experiments to narrow, revise, or falsify our claims.
4. We will not confuse coherence with goodness or persistence with worth.
5. We will build toward a framework that can return from error without collapsing into either dogma or vagueness.

> **Return to coherence under constraint.**
