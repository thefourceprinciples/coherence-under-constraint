# Coherence Under Constraint: A Unified Framework for Emergent Structure Across Systems

**Author:** Gage Fry  
**Version:** v0.1.0  
**Status:** Working paper scaffold  

---

## Abstract

Coherence Under Constraint (CUC) is an early-stage research framework for analyzing how stable structure emerges, persists, transforms, and collapses across complex systems. The framework proposes that durable structure depends on the interaction of three conditions: coherence, constraint, and throughput. Coherence refers to stable relationships among system components; constraint refers to boundaries that limit possible states; throughput refers to sustaining flows of energy, matter, information, attention, or resources.

CUC does not claim to replace existing domain sciences or introduce a new fundamental force. Instead, it provides a comparative structural vocabulary for examining recurring patterns across physical, biological, cognitive, technological, and social systems. This working paper defines the core terms, proposes a minimal formal backbone, identifies falsifiable research questions, and outlines an initial computational validation program.

---

## 1. Introduction

Many systems form stable structures only when dynamic processes become organized within limiting conditions. Phase transitions, biological self-organization, neural synchronization, stable network protocols, and durable social institutions are usually studied in separate fields with separate vocabularies.

CUC begins from the observation that these domains may share a structural pattern:

> Stable structure emerges when coherence is maintained under constraint through sustained throughput.

The framework is intended to support comparison, modeling, and hypothesis generation without erasing domain-specific mechanisms.

---

## 2. Core Thesis

The core thesis of CUC is expressed as:

```text
Structure = Coherence × Constraint × Throughput
```

This is not proposed as a final physical equation. It is a research heuristic and modeling scaffold.

A system is expected to persist when:

1. its internal relationships remain coherent enough to resist fragmentation;
2. its constraints are strong enough to bound possible states but flexible enough to permit adaptation;
3. its throughput meets or exceeds dissipative loss.

---

## 3. Definitions

### 3.1 Substrate

The substrate is the medium in which system dynamics occur. Examples include chemical media, physical fields, cellular tissue, neural networks, software architectures, social institutions, and symbolic systems.

### 3.2 Coherence

Coherence is the degree to which interacting elements maintain stable relationships across time. In some systems, this may be measured through synchronization, correlation, mutual information, phase alignment, or internal consistency.

A simple synchronization metric is:

```text
R(t) = |(1/N) Σ exp(iθᵢ(t))|
```

### 3.3 Constraint

Constraint is any boundary condition that limits possible system states. Constraints may be physical, biological, informational, computational, economic, social, legal, or symbolic.

### 3.4 Throughput

Throughput is the sustaining flow required for system persistence and adaptation. Depending on the domain, this may include energy, nutrients, computation, information, attention, resources, or communication.

### 3.5 Coherence Basin

A coherence basin is a stable attractor region in system state space where a pattern can persist under ordinary perturbation.

### 3.6 Threshold Transition

A threshold transition occurs when coherence, constraint, or throughput crosses a critical range and the system shifts into a different basin or mode of organization.

### 3.7 Interface Dominance

Interface dominance occurs when boundaries between systems become the primary sites of transformation, coherence gain, instability, or failure.

---

## 4. Minimal Formal Backbone

CUC begins with three formal conditions.

### 4.1 Coherence Threshold

```text
R(t) > R_critical
```

A structure can persist only when coherence remains above a domain-specific critical threshold.

### 4.2 Throughput Balance

```text
P_in ≥ P_diss
```

Sustaining input must meet or exceed dissipative loss.

### 4.3 Constrained Dynamics

```text
dX/dt = F(X,t) + ΓC(X)
```

System evolution depends on internal dynamics plus a constraint operator scaled by coupling strength.

---

## 5. Initial Predictions

CUC predicts that:

1. coherence fluctuations should increase near transition points;
2. stable structures should occupy measurable attractor basins;
3. interface regions should disproportionately shape transformation;
4. strong coherence without adequate throughput should produce brittleness or decay;
5. high throughput without effective constraint should produce dispersion;
6. constraint without coherence should produce fragmentation or coercive rigidity;
7. systems with searchable chronology and provenance should exhibit stronger memory coherence than systems without them.

---

## 6. Candidate Test Domains

### 6.1 Coupled Oscillators

Oscillator networks provide a minimal test domain for measuring coherence threshold behavior.

### 6.2 AI Memory Systems

AI conversation archives can be tested for retrieval quality, chronology preservation, contradiction detection, and provenance tracking.

### 6.3 Biological Organization

Cellular, neural, or ecological systems can be examined for relationships among coherence, boundary conditions, and sustaining throughput.

### 6.4 Social Systems

Institutions, organizations, and communities can be evaluated for the relation between shared coherence, constraints, resource flow, and durability.

---

## 7. Falsifiability

CUC should be revised or rejected where it fails to improve prediction, comparison, or explanation.

The framework is weakened if:

- coherence metrics do not correlate with persistence or transition in defined test systems;
- constraint changes fail to predict basin shifts;
- throughput balance does not improve persistence modeling;
- interface regions are not disproportionately important in tested systems;
- domain-specific theories consistently outperform CUC-derived predictions without useful generalization.

---

## 8. Research Program

The next stage is computational validation.

Planned steps:

1. implement a simple oscillator model;
2. define a minimal coherence score;
3. test coherence under changing constraints;
4. apply the same vocabulary to AI-memory datasets;
5. compare CUC-derived predictions against existing domain models.

---

## 9. Boundaries and Cautions

CUC is not a claim that coherence is inherently good. Harmful systems can be coherent. Rigid systems can persist. Captured systems can maintain internal alignment while producing destructive outcomes.

Therefore, CUC must be paired with evaluation criteria such as adaptability, reversibility, human benefit, transparency, and ethical context.

CUC is also not a claim that all systems are identical. It is a proposal that some systems share a structural grammar worth modeling.

---

## 10. Conclusion

Coherence Under Constraint proposes that durable structure depends on a three-part relationship among coherence, constraint, and throughput. The framework is currently conceptual and early-stage, but it is organized for computational testing and external critique.

The next milestone is not broader speculation. The next milestone is measurement.
